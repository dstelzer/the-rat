# Overview

Another gala. The 1% of the 1% of New Gothenburg are gathering behind closed doors to sip champagne and talk about investment opportunities. And they have no idea they're about to be robbed blind. A plucky band of rogues with their own grievances against the various billionaires has gotten together in the hopes of stealing everything that's not nailed down.

I'm not going to try to match the narrative voice of _The Bat_—I don't think I'd succeed, and a bad imitation is worse than no imitation at all. Instead, I'm going to try to imitate its themes (making the ultra-wealthy look not _evil_ but _ridiculous_) and its sense of humor (absurd people are in absurd situations but everyone in the story treats it with the utmost seriousness). The billionaires and their gala are the butt of the joke, but it's the players laughing at them, not the characters in the story.

# The Billionaires

## Count Vespertilion, the Host

A tall, thin, silver-haired man with an old-world sense of taste. It's unclear if he's actually a vampire or just leans into that aristocratic aesthetic, but none of his employees stay in his service for long without feeling their will to live sucked away.

Now he's down to a single long-suffering butler, who is expected to handle all the logistics of this party without assistance. Fortunately, this butler is quite resolute and uncomplaining. Fox has some kind of grudge against him, though the details are never specified.

"Count" isn't a hereditary title; it's an elected government position overseeing Vespertilion County, which contains the city of New Gothenburg. The fact that his campaign has been effectively unopposed for a decade now is sheer coincidence.

## Luna L. Meresankh, the Techbro

A spindly woman with an avant-garde hairstyle and way too much wearable tech. (The "L." stands for "Luna L. Meresankh".) Diversity win: this time, the techbro trying to disrupt your industry is a trans woman! Spider has a grudge against her for stealing his work and pushing him out of the startup they founded together.

She's your average Silicon Valley/Elizabeth Holmes type, the founder of a run-of-the-mill tech startup that unexpectedly made it big, who's now convinced that she's better and smarter than everyone else in the world. She's recently partnered with the Count to build her new project: Pyramid.AI, a revolutionary new system that will disrupt the very nature of social interaction!

(She wants to make it clear that the name has nothing to do with MLMs. She took a genetic test, found out she had some small fraction of Middle Eastern ancestry, and immediately adopted the whole Ancient Egyptian aesthetic while knowing nothing about actual present-day Egypt.)

(It is, however, absolutely a pyramid scheme.)

She and the Count are the hosts of this event, and their goal is to convince various wealthy investors that they should pour more money into the project. I talked with Mike Russo about what comeuppance the billionaires might actually face, in the end, and he pointed me to the example of Bernie Madoff: the one thing that will actually take a one-percenter down is revealing that they robbed other one-percenters. So while the *apparent* goal of the game is grabbing loot from the mansion and escaping, the true ending involves exposing fraud that implicates everyone in large-scale financial fraud.

## Dr Malatesta, the Unethical Experimenter

If this is going to be a sequel to _The Bat_, then it needs some recurring characters! Once again, Dr Malatesta is a classic mad scientist, a biologist who doesn't care what happens to his human subjects as long as they sign the non-disclosure agreement first. Rat's condition is due to one of his experiments, and any hope of compensation was SLAPPed down.

He's very excited about the results of his new research, though very cagey about what exactly those results are. Words like "in vivo genetic modification" and "newly-derived prions" are thrown about. It's certain to make him even more rich and famous than he already is, as long as nobody asks about his IRB approvals along the way.

## NAME TBD, the Refrigerator Magnate

I still need a name for him; I'm thinking something with lots of syllables starting with a Q, giving a sort of "affable old gentleman who spends a lot of money going to Africa and shooting elephants" vibe. The pun came first and the concept second. (I do like your "Woolett" suggestion.)

He's a Carnegie or Rockefeller sort, who made an astounding amount of money in the appliance business and is now throwing that money around in an attempt to whitewash his image. He wants his name to go down in history as a generous philanthropist and patron of the arts, the life of the party who's always quick with a joke even if it's a bit behind the times. And maybe then the biographers won't look too closely at the skeletons in his closet.

His twin daughters/nieces/something, Miss Constance and Miss Constance, have become well-known socialites (and businesswomen) in their own regard. Perhaps he's already well on his way to founding a proper American dynasty?

The exact skeleton-in-the-closet he's trying to cover up should be something so horrific it wraps around back to comedy. He also drove Jackdaw's small business to bankruptcy so he could corner the market, but that's just capitalism.

## NAME TBD, the Media Mogul

This one also still needs a name. My sister suggested Tension as a last name, which is a pun in its own right, but would also allow for A. Tension as a full name, for a second pun. I'm tempted.

She recently took over the New Gothenburg Post and instituted new priorities, ensuring that its reporting is well-focused and relevant to the everyday people of the city. Firing half the journalists and running long op-eds about how taxing the rich will destroy the economy is a step in the right direction. (Chameleon was one of those fired reporters.)

The obvious reference is Bezos taking over the Washington Post and refocusing it on "free markets and individual liberties", but I'm going to dig for some other inspirations as well. Character-wise, she yells everything in headlinese, which makes for some easy (and not quite so pointed) jokes.

# The Player Characters

For obvious heist-planning reasons, the team is using codenames instead of their real identities.

## Fox, the Mastermind

The player character, and pretty much a blank slate (so that the player can project whatever thoughts and traits they want). They give instructions to the rest of the team from a safe haven away from the premises; the parser interface represents getting messages from the others, and sending back commands in response.

It's implied they have a grudge against Count Vespertilion, but the reasons are left unstated. Given their excellent organizational skills, they would have made a great butler...

## Rat, the Infiltrator

The one who got the team together in the first place (and gave their name to the game). It's been infected with rat traits and behaviors as a result of Dr Malatesta's experiments, and got SLAPPed when it tried to get compensation. It's good at stealth and sabotage, but needs to hide whenever another person is in the room.

(Nobody actually knows much of anything about it: it never shows its face. The it/its pronoun usage might be a disguise or might be an identity. Chameleon has resisted the urge to dig.)

Actions: GO (move between rooms), SABOTAGE (cause a problem in the current room)

## Jackdaw, the Thief

A cold, cynical woman who's overtly only in this for the money. The rest of the team is buying her time while she breaks through security systems in the basement. She claims she used to be a legitimate businesswoman, but she was bankrupted by a large company moving in and operating at a loss until she shut down. Now she just wants enough money to not have to work for a few years.

She and Spider clearly get along well, and they probably know each other apart from this job. The others have wondered if those two intend to take the money and run. Hopefully they don't.

Actions: PROCEED (start working; earns points based on how long you keep the staff busy after giving this command), SCORE (how much did she get so far?)

## Spider, the Hacker

A talented engineer who sees this whole thing as a fascinating puzzle to be solved. He built the systems that the team is using to communicate (i.e. the parser interface) and is watching everything through security feeds, narrating descriptions of the rooms you go into. He also has a lot of personal hatred for LLM, who got rich off his tech and forced him out when he raised ethical concerns.

He and Rat seem to have a strangely mutually-one-sided rivalry. Jackdaw universally takes his side.

Actions: LOOK, EXAMINE, HACK (as the game progresses, he can open doors and devices for Rat)

## Chameleon, the Face

A self-proclaimed master of disguise who can slip into the party and distract the guests. She previously used her talents as an undercover reporter, revealing shocking truths that the elites don't want you to know. Then the Media Mogul took over the Post and she was immediately fired and blackballed.

Apart from caring deeply about her role and performance, it's hard to say anything specific about her. She flirts with Fox, but is she actually interested or just messing with them? She gives three or four mutually contradictory backstories. Mostly she seems to just enjoy lying for the fun of it—but _never_ in her published work.

Actions: GO, MINGLE (makes the guests follow her), DISENGAGE (makes the guests stop following her)

# The Staff

Vespertilion and LLM only have one reliable staff member on hand, which is woefully insufficient. Fortunately, he's quite competent.

## The Butler

An old man who's quite skilled at handling any problems as they come up. He can only attend to one thing at a time, though, and can't be in two places at once. Every problem you cause can be solved with exactly one of a curtain rod, a silver tray, and today's New Gothenburg Post.

The main gameplay loop is:
- Set everything up
- Tell Jackdaw to act
- Use Spider and Rat to distract the butler for as long as possible
- The amount of time Jackdaw had to act before the butler checked on the security system determines the score

Each phase of the game ends when you've acquired a certain amount of loot.

# Structure

The game is divided into five acts.

## Prologue: Getting Oriented

This is the tutorial. You only control Rat, in an isolated block of rooms outside the mansion. Two puzzles here get first Jackdaw and then Rat into the mansion proper, introducing the basic gameplay mechanics.

```
|
O-O-O
```

## Act 1: Getting In

Jackdaw is in the basement, robbing the wine cellar, while Rat is in the mansion above. It only has access to a few of the rooms, and only basic sabotage. None of the problems you cause require tools, just the butler going to specific places to fix them.

```
O-O
| |
O-O
| |
O-O
```

Once you've earned enough points in this phase, Jackdaw is able to get Spider connected to the mansion's wifi.

## Act 2: Getting Rich

The guests are now arriving, occupying the front few rooms (and making them inaccessible to Rat). But Spider can open all the doors, giving access to more of the map. Now you can do more elaborate sabotage that requires one of three tools (newspaper, curtain rod, silver tray) to solve.

```
  O-O
  | |
O-O-O-O
|     |
O     O
```

Once you've earned enough points in this phase, Spider is able to manipulate the guest list, getting Chameleon an official invitation.

## Act 3: Getting Out

The party is in full swing, and Chameleon is now in attendance. Jackdaw has gotten access to the guests' property and is robbing the whole stinkin' lot of them. The whole map is accessible, but Chameleon is able to block off any two rooms of the player's choice, letting them manipulate the topology. This is the most complex part of the game, where the player has to coordinate the skills of everyone on the team to succeed.

```
  O-O
  | |
O-O-O-O
| | | |
O-O-O-O
```

Once you've earned enough points in this phase, Jackdaw says it's time to extricate; you're all going to be filthy rich.

_But wait_—something happens, and a small puzzle requires Chameleon to leave her earpiece behind. This lets the player hear the discussions after the party, where Vespertilion and LLM reveal that none of the player's actions will significantly impact them. Everything is insured, and the butler will take the fall.

## Epilogue: Getting Even

As the butler cleans things away, he picks up the earpiece left behind, and takes it to a hidden servants' passage not accessible on the normal map. The puzzles in this part are quick and easy: it's a victory lap, not a hard endgame.

With the butler's help, you're able to obtain some dirt that will _actually_ hurt the billionaires if published. It was all a pyramid scheme after all! This gets you the true ending: you split the money six ways instead of five, but more importantly, all of your grudges are satisfied.

# Setting

I've got a general structure for the map, but the rooms are mostly vague right now. My current plan is to have it divided into a "Granite Wing" (Vespertilion's side: faux-Gothic architecture, lots of stone and wood, looks like a castle), a "Glass Wing" (LLM's side: stupidly impractical minimalism), and a "Gilded Wing" (gold all over to the point it just looks tacky). Each of them will have a different way to sabotage it.
